What this page is

Every morning a program watches finance YouTube channels, has Gemini (Google's AI) turn each new video into notes, and saves the notes to Google Drive. This page reads those notes, live Google Finance prices and your own holdings.

It shows what the analysts said today, how much they agree, how it fits what prices are doing, and whether their earlier calls worked. It reports; it never says buy or sell.

Use it in two minutes

  1. Read What matters today, above all the lines about your holdings.
  2. Glance at the headline tiles: mood, consensus, avoid.
  3. Before trusting a call, check Earlier calls right for that stock and the analyst's bar in Did the calls work?
  4. Click [watch] or a stock to hear the reasoning in the video yourself.

Words you will see

Call
One analyst's opinion on one stock in one video, such as "I like Nvidia here".
Bullish / bearish
Expects the price to rise / to fall.
Neutral / mixed
No clear direction / good and bad points together.
Ticker
A stock's short code: NVDA is Nvidia; GOOGL and GOOG are both Alphabet (Google).
S&P 500
An index of 500 large US companies: "the market".
Beat the S&P 500
Did better than the market over the same days.
Edge, pts
By how many percentage points a call beat the market, in the direction called.
bp
Basis point: one hundredth of a percentage point.
52-week high
The highest price of the past year.
P/E
Share price ÷ yearly profit per share.
10-year yield
The US government's 10-year borrowing rate; a fast rise often weighs on stocks.
Wall St. consensus
What professional analysts at banks and research firms say, collected by TipRanks.
Weight
The share of your portfolio's value in one stock.

What the colours mean

  • Blue bar: bullish calls, or an analyst whose calls beat the market.
  • Red bar: bearish calls, or an analyst whose calls lagged the market.
  • Grey bar: neutral or mixed calls.
  • +2.1%Green number: up or ahead. Red number: down or behind.
  • ConsensusAnalysts agree it is bullish. Split: both sides. Avoid: only negative. Lean: the rest.
  • Green edge on a note: good news that fits the calls.
  • Red edge: a warning or a clash, such as bullish calls while the price fell.
  • Amber edge: worth a closer look.
  • Grey edge: background.
