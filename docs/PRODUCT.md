# Econova: what it is and why it should exist

The product's heart on one page: what Econova is, for whom, why it matters, how it differs, and what it consists of.
Written for the founders and mentors. Last revised 26 November 2026, during the private beta.

## In one sentence

**Econova keeps score of what market experts say on TV and YouTube, and tells you where the ones worth listening to
stand on the stocks you own.**

## The problem

Self-directed investors learn about stocks mostly by watching: CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Schwab Network and
hundreds of YouTube channels. That content has three problems:

1. **Too much of it.** Dozens of hours a day. Nobody can watch it all, so people catch random fragments.
2. **It contradicts itself.** On the same day one guest calls a stock a buy and another calls it a sell.
3. **Nobody keeps score.** A guest who was badly wrong last month is back this week, just as confident. The viewer
   has no way of knowing who has been right.

The result is decisions made on whoever was loudest, most recent or most confident, not on who has been right.
In India the problem is now official: SEBI's 2024 rules on "finfluencers" exist because unaccountable stock tips
reach millions of new investors.

## Who it is for

**First customer:** a self-directed investor, 22 to 45, with 5 to 30 US stocks (directly, or through apps like
INDmoney or Vested from India), who watches finance videos but has a job and no time to watch everything.

What they want done, in their words: *"Tell me in a minute a day whether anything I own needs my attention, and
whether the people saying it are worth listening to."*

**Later customers:** finance creators who want a verified track record to show; brokers and media who want the
data (see "How it makes money").

## What Econova does

1. **Listens.** Every day it reads the new videos of the finance channels (an AI reads each video) and writes down
   every stock call: who made it, which stock, bullish or bearish, the reason in their words, and the exact moment.
2. **Keeps score.** Every call is checked against what the stock then did compared with the S&P 500. That builds a
   track record for every expert and every show: how often they are right, and by how much.
3. **Sums up.** For each stock it combines the experts' latest views (each expert gets one vote, better records count
   more, fresh views count most) with Wall Street's consensus into one view: **Bullish, Split or Bearish**, with how
   much evidence stands behind it and a short paragraph in plain words.
4. **Makes it yours.** Your stocks and what you hold, the day's big moves, what changed, the analysts you follow, and
   one short email after the market closes.
5. **Grades itself.** Econova publishes the track record of its own signal: when it said Bullish, did the stock beat
   the market over the next month? No black box, and no hiding the misses.

## What is new about it (the novelty)

- **The spoken word, structured.** Research reports and written ratings are already tracked (TipRanks does this
  well). What is said on air, the content retail investors actually consume, is not. Econova turns hours of video into
  rows of data: expert, stock, view, reason, timestamp.
- **Accountability by default.** Every call gets scored automatically, by the same rule, for everyone. An expert's
  confidence on screen counts for nothing; their record counts for everything.
- **Evidence you can check.** Every view links to the second in the video where it was said.
- **We grade ourselves too.** The signal's own hit rate is on a page anyone in the product can read.

## Why it matters to users

| Without Econova | With Econova |
|---|---|
| Hours of video, fragments remembered | A minute a day: what changed on your stocks |
| "Who is right?" is unknowable | Each expert's record, in stars, next to their call |
| The loudest view wins | Views weighted by track record and freshness |
| Buy and sell calls on the same day | One view per stock, with both sides quoted |
| Tips with no accountability | Every call and our own signal scored against the market |

## Competitors, and how we differ

| | What they do well | What they miss | Econova's difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TipRanks** | Ranks Wall Street analysts, bloggers, insiders; Smart Score | Written research only; dense and built for power users; most of it is paid | On-air and YouTube calls; one view per stock in plain words; we use their consensus as one input |
| **Seeking Alpha** | Long articles, quant ratings | Hours of reading; contributors are not scored on outcomes in a simple way | A minute a day; every view scored |
| **Google / Yahoo Finance** | Prices, charts, news, free | No opinions summed up, no accountability | The "why" behind the move, and who to trust on it |
| **YouTube itself** | The content | No memory: last month's wrong call is forgotten | The memory and the scorecard |
| **AI chat (ChatGPT, Perplexity)** | Answers any question | Answers without a track record; can be confidently wrong | Every statement traceable to a scored, dated call |
| **Tickertape, Smallcase (India)** | Indian screeners and baskets | Fundamentals, not expert opinion | Complementary: the expert layer |

**The edge we want:** to be *the* record of what market experts said and how it turned out. That edge compounds:
every day adds calls and outcomes, so a competitor starting a year later is a year of records behind. TipRanks did
exactly this for written research; nobody has done it for what is said on air.

## How we keep it honest (and legal)

- Econova **reports what experts said and how they did**. It never tells a person to buy or sell, and it does not
  know their finances. That is why the signal reads *Bullish / Split / Bearish* (a description of the experts) rather
  than *Yes / No* (an instruction), which in India could read as investment advice under SEBI's adviser rules.
- Every number is reproducible: the rules for scoring are written in the app's "How it works" page.
- Our own signal's misses are shown next to its hits.
- Members' holdings never leave their account; the engine never needs them.

## How it makes money (later; free during the beta)

1. **Free:** up to 5 stocks, the weekly email, the scorecards.
2. **Pro (about ₹299 or $5 a month):** unlimited stocks, the daily email, instant alerts when a view changes or a
   followed analyst makes a call, full call history.
3. **Data and API (B2B):** the scored record of on-air calls, for brokers, media and research desks.
4. **Verified record for creators:** a badge and page a creator can show, earned from their scored calls.

## How we will know it works

- **North star:** members who open the app or the email at least once a week (weekly engaged members).
- **Value:** stocks per member; share of members' stocks that have a view; feedback notes received.
- **Retention:** members still engaged after 4 weeks.
- **Quality:** the signal's hit rate and its edge over the S&P 500 (on the Track record page); hours since the last
  update.

## Risks we are watching

| Risk | What we do about it |
|---|---|
| Experts on TV are often wrong | That is the point: we show it. The value is knowing who is right, not trusting everyone |
| Small sample in the early months | Every record says how many calls it rests on; stars only after 3 scored calls |
| Regulation (SEBI, SEC) | Describe, do not advise; no personal recommendations; disclaimers on every page and email |
| Data rights (video, prices) | Short quotes with links to the source; licensed price data before charging money |
| Costs (AI reading, TipRanks quota) | Each video is read once for everyone; TipRanks calls spread across the month |

## Roadmap

- **Next:** instant alerts; insider trades from SEC filings; more channels (including the bigger YouTube creators);
  Indian stocks and Indian finance channels.
- **Later:** Pro plan; creator verified-record pages; the data API.
