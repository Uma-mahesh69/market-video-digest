      What do these words mean?
      Bullish
      The analyst expects the stock (or the market) to go up.
      Bearish
      The analyst expects it to go down.
      Neutral or mixed
      No clear view either way, or good and bad points together.
      S&P 500
      An index of the 500 largest US companies: the usual yardstick for "the market". A call is counted as right when the stock did better than it (bullish) or worse (bearish).
      Average edge
      How many percentage points an analyst's calls beat the S&P 500 by, on average. Above zero is good.
      Wall Street's rating
      The consensus of professional analysts at banks and brokers, from TipRanks: Strong Buy, Moderate Buy, Hold, Moderate Sell or Strong Sell.
      Price target
      The price those analysts expect in about 12 months. "+18%" means 18% above today's price.
      52-week (1-year) high
      The highest price in the past year. Far below it can mean a bargain or a company in trouble: the analysts' reasons tell you which.
      Weight
      A stock's share of what all your stocks are worth.
      Earnings
      The day a company reports its results. The price often moves a lot that day.
      P/E
      Price divided by a year's profit per share: how many years of today's profit the price pays for. Higher usually means more growth is expected.
